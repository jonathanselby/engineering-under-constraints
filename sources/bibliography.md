# Source Register

The repository uses short source IDs so historical chapters remain readable. Sources are selected for engineering relevance and provenance rather than volume.

## Commodore 64

**[C64-1] Commodore 64 Programmer's Reference Guide.** Commodore Business Machines, 1982. Contemporary technical reference for memory map, VIC-II, SID, CIA, I/O and BASIC/assembly interaction. Scanned copies are widely preserved in public computer archives.

## France

**[FR-1] Mirage 5 — origins and design.** Dassault Aviation historical archive. Documents the deliberate simplification of the Mirage III E layout, removal of radar and resulting fuel/cost trade-offs.  
https://www.dassault-aviation.com/en/passion/aircraft/military-dassault-aircraft/mirage-5/

**[FR-2] The Dassault Adventure historical timeline.** Dassault Aviation.

## Israel — Gabriel / Sa'ar

**[IL-1] IAI and the Security of Israel — company history.** Israel Aerospace Industries. Documents development of the Gabriel sea-to-sea missile from 1964.  
https://www.iai.co.il/about/history/

**[IL-2] Missile Boat Warfare: Israeli Style.** U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings, March 1986. Historical professional account of parallel Sa'ar/Gabriel development, integrated weapons-system preparation, training and maintenance.  
https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1986/march/missile-boat-warfare-israeli-style

**[IL-3] Professional Notes — Israeli missile-boat development.** U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings, September 1974. Contemporary account of small-vessel weight/space constraints, Gabriel development and ship/system integration.  
https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1974/september/professional-notes

**[IL-4] The Israeli Navy: 26 Years of Non-peace.** U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings, February 1975. Describes Sa'ar size, sensors/electronics and Gabriel integration.  
https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1975/february/israeli-navy-26-years-non-peace

## United States — smaller-service constraints

**[US-1] Expeditionary Advanced Base Operations.** United States Marine Corps, 2021. Defines the requirement for mobile, low-signature, relatively easy-to-maintain forces operating from austere temporary locations.  
https://www.marines.mil/Force-Design/Priorities/Article/2708120/expeditionary-advanced-base-operations-eabo/

**[US-2] Minotaur Mission System.** United States Coast Guard Acquisition Directorate. Documents government ownership, open architecture, cross-platform commonality and lifecycle-cost rationale.  
https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Our-Organization/Assistant-Commandant-for-Acquisitions-CG-9/Programs/Air-Programs/Minotaur-Mission-System/

**[US-3] Coast Guard Accepts First HC-130J Retrofitted With Minotaur.** USCG, 2017. Describes integration of multiple sensor streams and information sharing across platforms.  
https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Our-Organization/Assistant-Commandant-for-Acquisitions-CG-9/Newsroom/minotaur_110717/

## Soviet / Eastern Bloc

**[EE-1] Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-25.** National Museum of the United States Air Force. Describes the aircraft's simple-yet-functional design, vacuum-tube electronics, large engines and sparing use of advanced materials.  
https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/196331/AFmuseum/mikoyan-gurevich-mig-25/

**[EE-2] The Elbrus-2: a Soviet-era high performance computer.** Computer History Museum, 2013. Covers military/real-time requirements, architecture, high reliability goals and manufacturing limitations.  
https://computerhistory.org/blog/the-elbrus-2-a-soviet-era-high-performance-computer/

**[EE-3] BESM-6 supercomputer.** Computer History Museum collection. Notes long service life and production scale.  
https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/supercomputers/10/25/2380

## Source caveat

Some chapters necessarily combine institutional histories with later professional analysis. Where a source is also a participant or manufacturer, the chapter uses it primarily for design facts and avoids treating promotional language as independent proof of effectiveness.
